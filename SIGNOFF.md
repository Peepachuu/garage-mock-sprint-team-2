## Project Summary

During the project, the team has restyled the existing login page of the RMIT Garage boilerplate and the team has built a new introduction page which displays the team member’s photo, name, role and a small blurb. Post login redirection to the introduction page and route protection has also been implemented to prevent access without a valid session. During the progression of the project, work moved through a fixed role order, it started from the BA requirements to UX design mock-up and then back to BA for validation, before proceeding to the developers to build and independent testing and ending with the PM sign off. Before each handoff to the following role to begin work, details on what has been done was recorded in the task card. 

---

## Overall Team Reflection

**What went well:**
The role sequence was clear, and it held up well during the duration of the mock sprint. The requirements were written by the BA prior to any design work starting, and the design which was started after was able to validate against the requirement before any development having started, this meant that the developers were building on an approved specification rather than an evolving one. The acceptance criteria being written in to every card also meant that being “done” was actually verifiable, rather than being a judgment call, it made the final sign-off by the PM a matter of verifying against the requirement already established rather than re-reviewing the work.  

**What to improve next time:**
Towards the back half of the sprint, deployment and testing, they carried a tighter deadline compared to the first half, this left a more limited amount of time to absorb any delays and any unexpected roadblocks. A more even spread of the deadlines across the sprint would have given the deployment and testing phase as much breathing room as the design phase.  

**Findings:**
Every task which involved an unfamiliar tool or role ran between two to three times its inital time budget allocation, Figma, the BA role, Tailwind and Playwright all had the same pattern. While tasks which involved familiar tools came in at or under the time budget. During future sprints an explicit learning multiplier on time esimates should apply where a team memmber is using something for the first time, rather than treating the overrun as planning failure.
